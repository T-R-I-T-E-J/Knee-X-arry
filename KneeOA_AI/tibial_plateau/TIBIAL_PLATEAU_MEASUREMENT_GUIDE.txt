📋 WHAT THE SYSTEM DOES

Your request to the doctor:
"Divide the upper portion of tibia into two halves on the basis of fixed points,
 then measure the sizes of each half by using AI"

Solution:
The system automatically:
1. ✓ Detects the tibial plateau (flat upper surface of shinbone)
2. ✓ Identifies 3 fixed anatomical points:
   - Medial edge (inner boundary)
   - Lateral edge (outer boundary)
   - Central eminence (peak in center)
3. ✓ Draws a vertical division line through the center
4. ✓ Separates into MEDIAL HALF (left) and LATERAL HALF (right)
5. ✓ Measures Joint Space Width (JSW) in each half at 4 points
6. ✓ Returns clinical measurements with confidence scores

🎯 CLINICAL INTERPRETATION

Based on Mean JSW values:

GRADE 0 (Normal):
  - Medial JSW: > 5.0 mm
  - Lateral JSW: > 5.0 mm
  - Interpretation: No osteoarthritis

GRADE 1 (Doubtful):
  - Medial JSW: 4.0 - 5.0 mm  OR
  - Lateral JSW: 4.0 - 5.0 mm
  - Interpretation: Mild changes, may need follow-up

GRADE 2 (Minimal OA):
  - Medial JSW: 3.0 - 4.0 mm  OR
  - Lateral JSW: 3.0 - 4.0 mm
  - Interpretation: Definite OA changes, minimal severity

GRADE 3 (Moderate OA):
  - Medial JSW: 1.5 - 3.0 mm  OR
  - Lateral JSW: 1.5 - 3.0 mm
  - Interpretation: Significant cartilage loss

GRADE 4 (Severe OA):
  - Medial JSW: < 1.5 mm  OR
  - Lateral JSW: < 1.5 mm
  - Interpretation: Severe cartilage loss, bone-on-bone contact

ASYMMETRY ASSESSMENT:
  - If Medial JSW much less than Lateral JSW: Medial OA predominant
  - If Lateral JSW much less than Medial JSW: Lateral OA predominant
  - If both similar: Symmetrical OA

🖥️  SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

INPUT: Knee X-ray image (JPG, PNG, TIFF)
  │
  ↓
STEP 1: Image Enhancement
  - Normalize contrast
  - Denoise while preserving edges
  │
  ↓
STEP 2: Identify Knee Region (ROI)
  - Find bounding box of knee joint
  │
  ↓
STEP 3: Detect Tibial Plateau
  - Use adaptive thresholding
  - Identify bright bone area at top of tibia
  - Validate: Width >> Height (flat appearance)
  │
  ↓
STEP 4: Identify 3 Fixed Points
  - Medial Edge: min(x) of plateau contour
  - Lateral Edge: max(x) of plateau contour
  - Central Eminence: highest point in center x-range
  │
  ↓
STEP 5: Draw Division Line
  - Vertical line through central eminence
  - Separates into medial & lateral halves
  │
  ↓
STEP 6: Detect Femoral Surfaces
  - Find femoral condyle cortices above joint space
  │
  ↓
STEP 7: Measure JSW at 4 Points per Half
  - Calculate perpendicular distances
  - Convert pixels to millimeters (using calibration)
  - Compute statistics (min, max, mean, std dev)
  │
  ↓
STEP 8: Validate Measurements
  - Check for physiological plausibility
  - Calculate confidence score
  - Identify warnings
  │
  ↓
OUTPUT: Structured JSON with measurements, visuals, and clinical assessment

✅ VALIDATION RULES

The system automatically validates that:

1. POINT ORDERING: Medial < Central < Lateral (left to right)
2. JSW RANGE: 0.5 mm - 10 mm per measurement (physiologically plausible)
3. MEAN JSW CONSISTENCY: Std dev should be < 50% of mean
4. SYMMETRY: Medial and lateral JSW should be within 3mm of each other
5. IMAGE QUALITY: Sharpness score > 30/100 (acceptable for analysis)
6. DETECTION: At least 70% confidence in point identification

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

⚙️  CALIBRATION (Pixel to Millimeter Conversion)

The system needs to convert pixel measurements to clinical millimeters.

METHOD 1: Ruler Detection (Automatic - Best)
  - Detects ruler/scale markings on X-ray (if visible)
  - Measures spacing between 1cm marks
  - Calculates pixels per mm
  - Typical accuracy: ±1%

METHOD 2: Anatomical Reference (If No Ruler)
  - Uses tibial plateau width (normally 70-80mm in adults)
  - Estimates pixels per mm
  - Typical accuracy: ±10%

METHOD 3: Manual Calibration (If Available)
  - User provides known distance in pixels
  - Calculates conversion directly
  - Typical accuracy: ±2%
